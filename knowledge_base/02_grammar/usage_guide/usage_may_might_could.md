# English Usage Dilemma: Modal Verbs of Possibility: May, Might, Could

## Grammatical Rule & Distinction:
'May', 'might', and 'could' all express possibility in the present or future. 'Might' and 'could' express slightly more doubt or hypothetical condition than 'may'.

## Spoken Examples:
- 'It might rain later, so take an umbrella.'
- 'We could try that new restaurant if you like.'
- 'She may arrive late due to traffic.'

## Common Learner Mistakes to Correct:
Overusing 'maybe' at the start of every sentence instead of varied modal verbs.

## Tutor Recasting Directive:
If the student makes this mistake during conversation, gently recast the correct form in your reply and ask an engaging follow-up question.
