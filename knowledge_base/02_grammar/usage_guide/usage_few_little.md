# English Usage Dilemma: Few / A Few vs. Little / A Little

## Grammatical Rule & Distinction:
'Few' and 'a few' are used with countable plural nouns. 'Little' and 'a little' are used with uncountable nouns. 'A few' / 'a little' has a positive connotation (some, enough); 'few' / 'little' has a negative connotation (hardly any, insufficient).

## Spoken Examples:
- 'I have a few friends who speak fluent English.' (positive)
- 'Sadly, few people attended the talk.' (negative/almost none)
- 'I need a little time to finish this.'

## Common Learner Mistakes to Correct:
Using 'little' with plural countables (e.g. 'little people' when meaning 'few people').

## Tutor Recasting Directive:
If the student makes this mistake during conversation, gently recast the correct form in your reply and ask an engaging follow-up question.
