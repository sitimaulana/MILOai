# English Usage Dilemma: Must vs. Have to in Spoken English

## Grammatical Rule & Distinction:
'Must' conveys personal necessity or strong subjective feeling from the speaker. 'Have to' expresses external obligation (rules, laws, circumstances). In the past tense, 'had to' is required as 'must' has no past form.

## Spoken Examples:
- 'I must remember to call my mother.' (internal necessity)
- 'I have to wear a uniform at work.' (external rule)
- 'Yesterday I had to leave early.' (past obligation)

## Common Learner Mistakes to Correct:
Using 'must' for past obligations (e.g. 'Yesterday I must work' -> incorrect).

## Tutor Recasting Directive:
If the student makes this mistake during conversation, gently recast the correct form in your reply and ask an engaging follow-up question.
