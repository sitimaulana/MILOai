# English Usage Dilemma: Borrow vs. Lend

## Grammatical Rule & Distinction:
'Borrow' means to take something with the promise of returning it (take in). 'Lend' means to give something temporarily to someone else (give out).

## Spoken Examples:
- 'Can I borrow your pen for a moment?' (NOT: Can you borrow me...)
- 'Could you lend me some cash until tomorrow?'

## Common Learner Mistakes to Correct:
Using 'borrow' when meaning 'lend' (e.g. 'He borrowed me his jacket' is incorrect; should be 'He lent me his jacket').

## Tutor Recasting Directive:
If the student makes this mistake during conversation, gently recast the correct form in your reply and ask an engaging follow-up question.
